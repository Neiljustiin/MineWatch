01 — The Problem
!Illegal Mining Outpaces Manual Enforcement
Unlicensed mining (PETI) spreads across remote Indonesian forest at a scale no inspection team can patrol on foot.
Indonesia lost 721,000 hectares of forest to mining between 2001–2023 — 150,000 ha of it primary forest (Nusantara Atlas / Gaveau, 2024). Illegal mining sites stretch from Aceh to NTB, poisoning rivers with mercury, triggering floods, and destroying protected ecosystems.

A 70% rise in gold prices (2024–2026) is now fueling a fresh wave of illegal gold mining — while permit databases stay fragmented and out of date, and sites that get sealed simply reopen weeks later. In Jambi alone, officially licensed gold permits cover only ~36% of the area identified as PETI gold mining (21,879 ha licensed vs. 60,323 ha identified, KKI Warsi 2025) — leaving the rest completely unmonitored. Remote terrain, slow manual inspection and millions of hectares to cover mean most of this activity is discovered only after the damage is already done.

Why 5-day revisit mattersTwin-satellite Sentinel-2 constellation gives near-weekly refresh over Indonesia — fast enough to catch clearing before it expands.

Why human-in-the-loopFalse positives carry real cost (wrongly flagging a licensed operator). A trained analyst always makes the final call.
03 — Why It Works
★Four Design Choices That Set Us Apart
What separates MineWatch from a generic satellite image classifier.
⇄
Change detection, not static classification
Compares before/after image pairs to catch new clearing as it happens — not just "is there a mine here," which misses newly opened sites.
⚖
Flags risk — never issues a verdict
Output is "flagged for review" with a confidence score. A human analyst always makes the final call, avoiding false accusations.
✓
Validated against real enforcement cases
Cross-check methodology tested on 13 confirmed illegal-mining cases across Indonesia — not a synthetic benchmark.
🔓
Built on free, open, reproducible data
Sentinel-2 imagery (ESA) and official permit polygons (BIG Satu Peta) are both public — no proprietary lock-in.
Competitive Edge
No existing entry in this competition applies satellite change-detection to illegal mining specifically. Every dataset is free & public, the approach is backtestable against real enforcement cases, and the same pipeline is directly scalable to other countries facing the same problem — the Amazon, the Congo Basin, and beyond.
